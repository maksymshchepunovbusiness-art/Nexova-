# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Non-technical owners of small local businesses in Poland and Czechia — restaurants and food producers, barbershops and beauty professionals, dental and medical practices, auto workshops and tradespeople, small shops and wholesalers. They arrive needing a first website or a redesign, and they are comparing three options: a cheap freelancer, a do-it-yourself site builder, and a traditional agency. They do not evaluate on technology — they evaluate on trust, clear pricing, and whether the provider understands their line of work. Their real fears: overpaying, being ghosted after payment, endless delays, talking to a call center instead of the person doing the work, and getting a generic template that doesn't fit their business. A secondary surface (a client care area) may exist after the engagement, but the marketing site drives that first decision.

## Product Purpose

Nexova's marketing site exists to turn local business owners into paying clients — to make them trust Nexova and start a project (send an inquiry / request a quote), not merely to raise awareness. Success looks like a visitor understanding the offer in seconds, trusting the person behind it, and completing a contact/booking action with minimal friction. The site serves four languages: Polish (primary), Czech, English, and Ukrainian.

## Brand Personality

Modern, trustworthy, human, direct. Confident and clean, but warm and plain-spoken — deliberately positioned between the disappearing cheap freelancer and the faceless expensive agency. It speaks the client's language with no tech jargon, stays concrete and honest, and reassures at every step. In one line: the reliable maker you can talk to directly.

## Anti-references

Avoid two traps at once. First, the cold corporate-agency look (faceless, navy-gray, stock handshakes, dense tabular layouts) that feels expensive and impersonal. Second, the generic Silicon-Valley / AI-SaaS look (cream/sand backgrounds, gradient text, hero-metric templates, identical icon-tile grids, tiny uppercase eyebrows) — it reads as "for tech startups" and alienates a local pierogi maker or plumber. Also avoid cheap-freelancer signals (clutter, loud discount banners, template vibes) and any technical jargon in copy.

## Design Principles

- Lead with trust, clarity, and "I understand your business," expressed through clean modern design and plain language — not corporate stiffness, not startup flash.
- Every page moves the local owner toward one clear action (request a quote / contact). Reducing confusion and friction outranks decorative flourish.
- Transparent pricing and a concrete bug-fix guarantee are the core trust signals — show them prominently, never bury them.
- Speak the client's language: benefits and reassurance over features and jargon; short sentences; concrete promises.
- Accessibility is foundational (the audience skews older and mobile): strong contrast, mobile-first, large tap targets, keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion support from the start — not a finishing pass.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the firm floor (≥4.5:1 body text), AAA where feasible; full keyboard navigability; generous touch targets; mobile-first layouts; complete `prefers-reduced-motion` support for all motion; and correct multilingual typography (Polish, Czech, and Ukrainian glyphs) so no language ever renders with broken characters.
